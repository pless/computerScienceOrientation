Pinging the Internet

In this assignment, you will explore how messages move across the Internet. Every computer on the Internet has an address, called an IP address. You will use free online tools to look up addresses, to send test messages, and to see the path a message takes around the world.

The questions below are your guide for the exploration. At the end, you will write a very short post on your blogs.gwu.edu blog, like the previous labs.

The tool

Your tool for this assignment is dnschecker.org/all-tools.php. The site has many tools, and you need only a few of them:

  • What Is My IP Address — shows the address that your computer uses on the Internet
  • Ping — sends a short message to an address and measures the time, in milliseconds, until the answer comes back
  • IP Location Lookup — shows where in the world an IP address is
  • Traceroute — shows each step that a message takes on its way to an address
  • Domain to IP Lookup — finds the IP address for a website name

Try each of these tools before you start the questions. Also look at some of the other tools on the site.

The same tools on your own computer

Every one of these tools is also built into your computer. Running them yourself is more fun, and it gives you a second answer to compare with the website. Open your terminal (Mac: the Terminal app; Windows: Command Prompt), type a command, and press Return. On a Mac, press Control-C to stop a command that keeps running.

Replace google.com or 8.8.8.8 in the commands with whatever address you want to look up. On a Chromebook, press Ctrl+Alt+T to open a terminal; it has ping and tracepath, but not the others, so use the website for those.

Questions to work through

Take a screenshot each time you see something interesting. You will use your screenshots in your post at the end.

  1. What is your computer's IP address?
  2. Use the Ping tool to find the IP addresses for cs.seas.gwu.edu, emse.seas.gwu.edu, and google.com. Are these addresses the same or different? Why?
  3. How long does it take the Ping tool to get an answer from google.com?
  4. Your computer has its own ping program. Open your terminal (Mac: the Terminal app; Windows: Command Prompt; Chromebook: press Ctrl+Alt+T). Type ping google.com and press Return. On Mac and Chromebook, press Control-C after a few lines to stop it. Is your time faster or slower than the time from the website? Why are the two times different?
  5. www.tsinghua.edu.cn is Tsinghua University, in Beijing. How long does it take the Ping tool to get an answer from www.tsinghua.edu.cn? What is its IP address?
  6. Use the IP Location Lookup tool on your own IP address. Where does it say that you are?
  7. Where is google.com? Do you think that the answer would be the same if you lived in California?
  8. Where is www.tsinghua.edu.cn?
  9. Use the Traceroute tool on www.tsinghua.edu.cn. It shows the IP addresses on the path to Tsinghua, one for each step. Use the IP Location Lookup tool on some of these addresses. Which countries does the message go through? (Bonus: open the submarine cable map. Which cable do you think the message crossed?)
  10. What is the longest ping delay that you can find? How did you find it?
  11. Pick a target: your home, or a famous place like the Statue of Liberty or the pyramids in Cairo. Find an IP address with a location that is close to your target. Hint: think of organizations near the target, like a university, a city government, or a local news site. Use Domain to IP Lookup on their website names. Then use IP Location Lookup on the results. Some answers will surprise you: many websites live in data centers far from their owners. How close to the target can you get?

The newer Internet: IPv6

The addresses above have four numbers, like 101.6.15.66. This format is called IPv4. The world ran out of new IPv4 addresses years ago. The new format, IPv6, has much longer addresses, like 2402:f000:1:402:101:6:15:66. The Internet is still in the middle of this change. Find out who has made the change:

  1. Open test-ipv6.com. The test starts by itself. What score do you get? Does your network support IPv6?
  2. Use the IPv6 Compatibility Checker. Does google.com support IPv6? Does www.tsinghua.edu.cn? Now find a famous site that does not support IPv6. (Hint: try gwu.edu and github.com…)
  3. Use Ping IPv6 to ping google.com. Is it faster or slower than the IPv4 ping in question 3?

Write your post

Now that you have explored, write a very short post on your blog — the same blog as last week. Tell us the one most interesting fact that you found. For example:

  • “The Yosemite Park webpage is served from a computer in New York City.”
  • “Ping times from GWU are slow!”

Any one fact works. Then defend your claim with screenshots from the tools you used.
